01

We began by reading your Daybook

Not a questionnaire, not a demonstration. We studied the workbook your bursars actually keep, and it changed what we proposed.

Daybook Sec is not an informal spreadsheet. It is a genuine OHADA cash-analysis ledger built in Excel with real discipline: every receipt and payment entered once in the cash book, then again in the column that classifies it, month after month, across a July-to-June financial year.

Your accounts already carry proper OHADA codes — 7020000 Tuition 1st Cycle, 7022000 State Subvention — and your expense columns follow the statutory classes in order. Your monthly summary already reports Budget · Monthly · Brought Forward · Cumulative · Balance, and your profit-centre block tracks both the income and the cost of the canteen, the uniforms, the bookshop and the phone booth.

Our conclusion

You do not need to be taught accounting. You need the typing to stop, and the Archdiocese needs to see the result without waiting for a file to be emailed. We are not proposing to replace your daybook. We are proposing to fill it in for you.

02

Your own budget names the problem

We did not have to guess where the losses are. Four lines in your expense structure say it plainly — and they are budgeted for every year, as a matter of course.

COLUMN DG Cash Shortages

Money is handled by many hands, in cash, and some of it does not arrive. This is anticipated in the budget.

COLUMN DE Bad Debts

Fees charged and never recovered, written off at the close of the year.

COLUMN EH Provision For Uncollected Fees

And more expected never to arrive at all — provided for in advance.

COLUMN EI Provision For Contingencies

Together with whatever else the year brings that the books cannot yet name.

What else the structure reveals

  • Every figure is entered by hand, twice. Once in the cash book, once in the analysis column — across 155 columns, for twelve months.
  • The workbook lives on one machine. The Archdiocese cannot see a school's position until someone remembers to send the file.
  • Consolidation is manual. Combining institutions means opening several workbooks and adding them up — so the diocesan picture is always late.
  • Parents must travel with cash. Queues at the bursary, cash in a drawer, a receipt book — which is exactly where Cash Shortages comes from.
  • Relatives abroad cannot pay at all, though they fund a large share of school fees in this country.
03

What changes

For the parent

They open a page, find the school, identify the student, see the exact balance, and pay by MTN or Orange Money from wherever they are — a village, Douala, or Maryland. A receipt arrives immediately, with a code anyone can verify.

For the bursar

The payment posts itself: to the student's fee record, to the cash book, and to the right analysis column. The daybook fills in as money moves. At month end the summary is already written, and the workbook can still be exported in exactly the shape they know.

For the Archdiocese

Every institution's position, current, in one place: income and expenditure by OHADA class, surplus or deficit, cash position, what is owed and how long it has been owed. Schools compared with schools. And, on the same page, the hospitals.

The point of it

Cash that never enters a drawer cannot go missing from one. Fees that can be paid from a phone are more likely to be paid. And a deficit seen in March is a problem; a deficit seen in June is a loss.

05

When the power fails and the internet does not come

A system that only works on a good day is no use in a bursary. This one is built for the days that are not good.

  • The school system runs on the school's own server, on its own network. Admissions, fees at the counter, the daybook and receipts all continue with no internet at all.
  • No special connection is needed — no fixed address, no public link, nothing opened to the outside. A phone hotspot is enough.
  • Parents can still pay while the school is offline, because the balances were published in advance. The payment reaches the school when it comes back.
  • Nothing is lost or counted twice. Counter payments and online payments reconcile when the connection returns.
  • The bursar can see the connection — “last synced 4 minutes ago · 3 payments waiting” — so silence is never mistaken for safety.
Stated honestly

After a long outage a published balance may be a little out of date, so a parent could pay for a bill just settled at the counter. The page always shows the time the balance was taken, never accepts more than is owed, and any overlap becomes a credit on the student's account. A duplicate can be corrected; a parent turned away after travelling cannot.

06

What we will not do

A proposal about money should be as clear about its limits as its promises.

We never hold your money

Payments go straight into each institution's own account. We are not a bank and do not act as one. Our fee is invoiced to you separately, in the open.

Patient records never leave a hospital

Only financial and operational totals reach the Archdiocese. No patient row, ever, under any circumstance.

We do not take over your books

Each institution keeps its own chart of accounts and remains the authority on its own ledger. We consolidate; we do not overwrite.

Your work stays yours

The daybook remains in the shape your bursars know, and can be exported at any time. If you left us tomorrow, you would leave with your accounts intact.

08

Beyond schools

The Archdiocese does not only run schools, so the platform does not only understand schools.

Hospitals, a press, a farm, a guest house — each connects as an institution and reports in the same shape. A finance council can then compare a college with a clinic without translating between two sets of books, because both arrive expressed in OHADA classes.

Institution Reports financially And operationally
School Income and expenditure by OHADA class · surplus or deficit · cash position · receivables and payables with ageing · budget against actual Enrolment, fee collection rate, arrears
Hospital Admissions, bed occupancy, patient debt
Business Turnover, gross margin, stock value

Hospitals join for reporting first. Patient billing is a different problem from school fees — decided at the point of care, often involving insurance — and we would rather do it properly later than badly now.

What success looks like

  • A measurable share of fees paid without anyone queueing at a bursary.
  • Cash Shortages falling, because less cash is handled.
  • Arrears visible in the term they arise, not at the year's close.
  • A diocesan position available on any day of the year, not after it.
  • Bursars who would refuse to go back to typing.
One honest note on figures

The workbook we studied is a template: it carries your accounts and your budget structure, but no amounts. We have therefore quoted no monetary figures anywhere in this proposal. When the Archdiocese shares real figures, we will model the effect properly — we would rather show you nothing than show you numbers we invented.
